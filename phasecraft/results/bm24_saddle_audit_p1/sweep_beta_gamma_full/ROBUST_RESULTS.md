## What "Seed Failure" Means

In this report, "seed failure" does NOT mean that the BM24/Krawczyk seed root failed certification.

All 600 seed saddles were certified.

Seed failure means:

1. The certified BM24 seed saddle still exists as a local saddle.
2. But its exponent no longer matches the exact finite-n exponent:
   `seed_gap = full_conv2_seed - lambda_abs`.
3. When `|seed_gap|` becomes large, the seed is no longer a good candidate for the controlling saddle.
4. In many such regions, a competitor saddle is closer to exact lambda_abs than the seed.

So "seed failure" means "failure as the asymptotically controlling / finite-n explanatory saddle", not "failure to find or certify the saddle".

The most reliable diagnostic in this sweep is `seed_gap`, because lambda_abs is computed from the exact finite-n sum. The competitor search helps identify possible replacement saddles, but random-start competitor discovery is not a global completeness proof.

## Physical-Admissibility Check: Probability Cannot Exceed 1

The exact success probability satisfies `0 <= p_succ <= 1`, hence the physical exponential rate must satisfy

`lambda = log(p_succ) / n <= 0`.

Therefore any individual saddle with

`full_conv2_exponent = Re Phi_M + phi_pref_conv2 > 0`

cannot be the uncancelled physical leading contribution to the original probability. Such a saddle may be a valid algebraic/Krawczyk-certified root of the saddle equations, but it must either:

1. have zero intersection with the original integration contour,
2. live on the wrong logarithmic sheet,
3. cancel against other saddle contributions, or
4. be irrelevant to the physical success probability despite having large algebraic Re Phi.

This is why largest algebraic `Re Phi` is not, by itself, a physical dominance criterion.

The physically meaningful checks are:

- whether the saddle exponent is compatible with `lambda_abs <= 0`,
- whether its exponent matches the exact finite-n `lambda_abs`,
- whether its Im Phi is on the correct phase sheet, typically Im Phi = 0 mod 2*pi for the real positive contribution,
- and ultimately whether its thimble has nonzero intersection with the original contour.

This affects the interpretation of the competitor results. It does not invalidate the seed-gap diagnostic. A large seed gap still shows that the BM24 seed is not explaining the exact sum. But it does mean that high-Re competitors with impossible positive exponents should not be interpreted as physical replacements.

## Interpretation

### 1. Near the original p=1 beta, the old conclusion survives.

At beta = 0.5434, the seed is accurate through gamma ~= -1.8 and starts failing by gamma ~= -1.9.

This matches the prior branch-continuation result: the loss of dominance is an anti-Stokes-type crossover to a real near-trivial competitor branch.

### 2. The conclusion is not global over beta.

The bands beta ~= 0.8 to 1.3 and beta ~= 5.0 to 5.5 show fast seed failure. In these bands the seed exponent is already far below exact lambda_abs at modest gamma.

### 3. There are trivial / symmetry bands where the seed is essentially exact.

Beta near pi/2, pi, and 2pi is extremely stable in this diagnostic. At beta=pi and beta=2pi, the seed gap is essentially zero everywhere on the sampled grid.

### 4. Algebraic high-Re competitors are everywhere but are not enough.

Large Re-Phi competitors appear at most grid points. For example:

- Delta Re > 1 at 397 / 600 points
- Delta Re > 2 at 236 / 600 points
- Delta Re > 4 at 54 / 600 points

But many of these are high-Re log-sheet artifacts. Therefore, largest algebraic Re Phi alone is not reliable as a physical dominance test.

If `full_conv2_exponent > 0`, the candidate is automatically non-physical as an uncancelled contribution, since it would imply an exponentially growing probability. Such candidates are useful as diagnostics of the algebraic saddle landscape but should be filtered out when making claims about the controlling physical saddle.

## Reliability Assessment

Reliable:

- The seed exists and is locally certified at all 600 sampled points.
- The seed-gap diagnostic is reliable as a finite-n check, because it compares against the exact finite-n exponent lambda_abs.
- The qualitative phase structure is robust: the bad beta bands and stable beta bands are large, not single-point artifacts.

Less reliable / needs follow-up:

- The competitor search is not a global proof. It used 250 random starts per point, which is strong but not exhaustive.
- The finite-n comparison uses n=18..24. This is useful and consistent with earlier n=40 checks, but true asymptotic control should be confirmed with larger n at representative beta values.
- Physical contour dominance still requires Picard-Lefschetz / intersection-number evidence. The seed-gap diagnostic tells us when the seed is not explaining the exact sum; it does not alone prove which thimble is active.
- Any competitor with positive full exponent must be rejected as a physical uncancelled saddle, because `p_succ` cannot exceed 1.

Recommended next checks:

1. Refine beta bands near 0.7 to 0.8 and 1.3 to pi/2.
2. Run n=30..40 checks at representative points:
   - beta=0.9, gamma=-1.0
   - beta=1.1, gamma=-1.6
   - beta=5.0, gamma=-1.6
   - beta=0.5434, gamma=-1.83
3. Track the replacement branch continuously in beta as well as gamma.
4. Compute Im Phi modulo 2pi for the replacement branch in the bad beta bands.
